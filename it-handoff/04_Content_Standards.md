# Content Standards — Grower X-Ray Quality Report

Tone of voice and boilerplate-adaptation rules: how fixed paragraphs should read differently depending on whether a grower's numbers look good, bad, or flat.

Status: **DRAFT — in progress**

## Overall tone

Warm, factual, non-judgmental, and supportive — regardless of how good or bad the grower's numbers are. The report is a transparency tool ("something most growers never see"), not a scorecard used to pressure growers. This holds even when a grower's results are poor.

## Rule: Season-on-season narrative — good news vs. bad news

The season-on-season comparison sentence (see Business Rules for when this section appears/is omitted) has two tone variants depending on direction of change:

**Improved (Yarranabee-style):**
> "Your reject rate has dropped from [old]% to [new]%, a [diff] percentage point reduction year on year!"
- Celebratory, exclamation mark, credits the grower directly (Yarranabee's version continues "Genuine credit to the work going into your paddocks.")

**Got worse:**
> "Your reject rate increased from [old]% to [new]%, a [diff] percentage point rise year on year. Seasons vary for all sorts of reasons, and we're happy to help work through what changed if that would be useful."
- Factual and neutral. **No exclamation mark.** State the numbers plainly, don't editorialise or apologise on the grower's behalf.
- Pairs with a **supportive, forward-looking line** rather than anything that reads as blame — mirrors the standard "What This Means for You" closing ("We are happy to talk it through if it helps.")

## General writing style guidelines (observed patterns across the shipped report)

These aren't explicit rules Cody dictated, but consistent patterns across every paragraph of the Yarranabee report — useful for IT/whoever drafts new template copy to stay consistent:

- **Direct second-person address.** Always "your hay," "your paddocks," "your reject rate" — never "the grower's" or third person.
- **"We/you" partnership framing**, not "us vs. them." Even when explaining a reject, the company frames it as something *it* catches and stops on the grower's behalf ("every reject is one we have stopped before it reaches your customer"), not something the grower did wrong.
- **Reassurance before criticism.** Cause explanations lean toward external/structural factors (ground conditions, plant-side issues) over grower fault wherever plausible — e.g. "rather than anything wrong with the hay."
- **Plain language, minimal jargon.** Technical terms (ARGT, slicer plant) are used but immediately contextualised in-sentence rather than assumed knowledge.
- **Numbers are always precise**, never rounded away — exact bale counts, one-decimal percentages, exact percentage-point differences.
- **Exclamation marks are rare and earned** — reserved for genuinely strong positive results (see season-on-season rule above). Not used in neutral or negative contexts.
- **Every page-1 section that could read as criticism closes with an open door** — "We are happy to talk it through if it helps," repeated in slightly different words across sections. This should be treated as a near-mandatory closing pattern for any adaptive/negative-leaning paragraph.
- **Transparency framing in the intro** ("something most growers never see") sets the tone for the whole report — it's presented as a value-add/gift of information, not a compliance obligation.

## Rule: Branded banners stay constant regardless of performance

The navy branded banners (e.g. "Feeding the animals that feed the world" / thank-you message) are relationship and gratitude messaging, **not** a performance readout. Confirmed with Cody (2026-07-08): banner wording does **not** soften, change, or otherwise adapt based on whether the grower had a good or bad season — it stays the same warm/thankful tone for every grower, every time.
